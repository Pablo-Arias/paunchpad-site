For Ableton Live 12 · Launchpad Pro MK3

Paunchpad: in-depth Ableton controller scripts, tailored for live performance.

Built by Pablo Arias Sarah · made for Ableton Live

Paunchpad is an Ableton Live remote script for the Launchpad Pro MK3. It takes over every pad and light, and turns the Launchpad into the controller you design. You get Session mode, velocity-sensitive faders, drum and melodic sequencers, Note mode, a Custom mode of 64 slider pads and an LFO mode of 64 slow LFOs. You choose which button opens what: up to ten modes on the five top-row buttons, each pressed or with Shift. Everything is saved with your Live Set. It's almost 6,000 lines of Python, tested by nearly 800 automated checks.

Tested with Ableton Live 12.4.6 Suite · macOS 26.6 · Launchpad Pro MK3 · details

Paunchpad puts the Launchpad in programmer mode. In that mode the script, rather than the Launchpad's firmware, decides what every pad does and how it lights. On top of that it builds everything below, laid out like the Launchpad Pro MK3's own modes wherever it can. Out of the box, the top-row buttons open two drum sequencers, three melodic sequencers, Note mode and Custom mode, but that's only a starting point: you can rearrange them and add more (chapter 4).

▶

Session mode

Launch clips and scenes, move the session box, record, and arm, mute or solo tracks: the stock Launchpad Session mode.

≡

Faders

Volume, pan, sends and device parameters on the grid. The harder you hit, the faster a fader glides, and a pad pressed twice gives finer steps.

◼

Drum sequencers

Two independent step sequencers for Drum Racks: 32 steps, per-step velocity, probability, mutation and micro-timing, note repeat and throw pads.

♪

Melodic sequencers

Three independent Push-style sequencers that follow Live's key and scale, with pages, loops, randomise and key changes that carry the playing clips along.

⌨

Note mode

Play the selected track: chromatic or in-key layouts, overlaps, and a Drum layout for Drum Racks, as on the LPP MK3.

⧉

Custom mode

64 slider pads that you link to any control in Live. They light in the colour of the control's track and follow it when you move it with the mouse.

How I play itLive, I play Paunchpad with 3 melodic step sequencers and 2 drum sequencers, each on its own track, plus Custom mode: a matrix of slider pads linked to the controls I modulate (filters, sends, effects). That's the default layout: Note and Chord open the drums, Custom, Sequencer and Projects the melodies, and Shift + Custom the sliders.
Make it yoursEverything is customisable. Any mode can go on whichever top-row button you want, as a press or with Shift, and you can add extra sequencers. You set it in one small settings file, Config.py; see chapter 4. Every speed, colour and behaviour is open in the script's code too.
Good to knowEvery sequencer remembers its own track, pattern and view. What you set up (track locks, Custom mode links and values) is saved in the Live Set, so it comes back when you reopen it.

▶ ARTR on YouTube  More about me

The Launchpad Pro MK3 is a wonderful instrument, designed to be many things at once: a standalone sequencer, a controller for any DAW, and a great Session controller for Ableton Live. Paunchpad makes a different trade-off: it gives up standalone use for depth in one place. Paunchpad takes over the Launchpad in programmer mode to create an in-depth Ableton controller, specially designed for live performance. Here's where that changes how you play:

Launchpad Pro MK3

Its own sequencer

The built-in sequencer is a full standalone step sequencer: it runs even without a computer, alongside Ableton's clips.

With Paunchpad

Sequencing inside Ableton

Every step you place is a note in a Live clip on the sequencer's track. The sequencers follow the clip that's playing, and Session mode, clips and sequencers share one timeline. See Drum and Melodic sequencer.

Launchpad Pro MK3

Pads that play as you pick

Picking a drum sound plays it, which is great for finding sounds by ear. The alternative, selecting the step first and then the sound, takes more presses: 16 per bar for a four-on-the-floor kick instead of 8.

With Paunchpad

Silent selection for the stage

With Record off, the drum pads select sounds silently. Pick the kick, tap four steps, done. Turn Record on when you want the pads to play.

Launchpad Pro MK3

Settings per step

Velocity, probability and mutation apply to the whole step, so everything on a step shares them.

With Paunchpad

Settings per sound, per step

Velocity, probability, mutation and micro-timing are set for the selected sound on the selected step. The other sounds on that step keep their own values.

Launchpad Pro MK3

Mutes in the mixer

Individual drum sounds are muted in Live, on the Drum Rack. This was something I was used to doing from the pads with my Launchpad95 scripts.

With Paunchpad

Mute any sound from the pads

The Mute button (the LPP's Print to Clip) mutes and unmutes the selected sound, or hold it and tap pads. Muted sounds keep their steps and show dim red.

Launchpad Pro MK3

Custom modes made in Components

Custom modes are designed in Novation Components and live on the grid; the scene buttons and Live's blue hand aren't part of them. Their controls send fixed messages, so what they control can't be changed from within the mode. I had rebuilt the blue hand in Max for Live.

With Paunchpad

A Custom mode that uses every button

Paunchpad's Custom mode uses the scene buttons for rows and the buttons below the pads for columns. Link any pad to any Live control right from the Launchpad (Quantise + pad), and relink it whenever you like. In the drum sequencer, the sound controls follow the selected sound's device.

Launchpad Pro MK3

A firmware you play

The modes live in the Launchpad's firmware, polished and ready to play, but not meant to be changed.

With Paunchpad

Open and fully customisable

Paunchpad is a plain Python remote script, built on the Pro MK3's programmer mode. Choose what each button opens in Config.py, and change any speed, colour or behaviour in the code.

Thank you, NovationPaunchpad only exists because the Launchpad Pro MK3 is such good hardware, and because Novation gives it a documented programmer mode that lets anyone build on it.
Chapter 3

Getting started

Install the script once, pick it in Live's settings, and lay out your Set so that the sequencers find their tracks.

  1. Install. Put the Paunchpad folder in ~/Music/Ableton/User Library/Remote Scripts/. (Paunchpad isn't released yet, so there's no download for now. I'll release it if there's interest: subscribe to show yours and to hear when it's out.)
  2. Select it in Live. Open Settings → Tempo & MIDI and set:
    SettingValue
    Control SurfacePaunchpad
    InputLaunchpad Pro MK3 (LPProMK3 MIDI)
    OutputLaunchpad Pro MK3 (LPProMK3 MIDI)
  3. Set the ports in the MIDI Ports list:
    • LPProMK3 MIDI input: Remote ✔ and Track ✔, so the drum pads can sound on their tracks.
    • LPProMK3 MIDI output: Remote ✔, Track ✘, Sync ✘.
    • LPProMK3 DAW (input and output): everything off. Don't load the stock Launchpad_Pro_MK3 script at the same time.
  4. Lay out your Set. Each top-row button opens a sequencer that starts on its own track. Put two drum tracks (MIDI tracks with a Drum Rack) first, then three melodic MIDI tracks:
    ButtonOpensStarts on
    NoteDrum sequencer ATrack 1
    ChordDrum sequencer BTrack 2
    CustomMelodic sequencer ATrack 3
    SequencerMelodic sequencer BTrack 4
    ProjectsMelodic sequencer CTrack 5
    To move a sequencer to another track, select that track while the sequencer is shown. This is the default layout: to put other modes on these buttons, see chapter 4.
Use the MIDI portIn programmer mode the Launchpad only talks on its MIDI port, not the DAW port. Live's dropdowns cut the names short ("Launchpad Pro M…"), so check the Ports list afterwards: the entry renamed "Paunchpad Input/Output" must be the MIDI one.
TipFor the lowest latency, use wired audio with a 128-sample buffer. Bluetooth headphones add around 200 ms.

Chapter 4

Make it yours

Paunchpad is built to be shaped around the way you play. Put any mode on any of the five top-row buttons (Note, Chord, Custom, Sequencer, Projects), as a press or with Shift. You can also add more sequencers and choose which track each one starts on.

How to change the buttons

  1. Open Config.py in the Paunchpad folder (~/Music/Ableton/User Library/Remote Scripts/Paunchpad/) with any text editor, such as TextEdit or VS Code. In TextEdit, choose Format → Make Plain Text first.
  2. Choose what each button opens, in BUTTONS. BUTTONS is the list that decides which mode opens when you press one of the five top-row buttons (Note, Chord, Custom, Sequencer, Projects). Each button has two lines: one for pressing it on its own, and one for pressing it while holding Shift. That gives you ten places to put a mode.

    Each line reads button : mode. For example:

    'Shift + Custom':    'custom mode',   # hold Shift and press Custom: Custom mode opens

    To change what a button does, change only the mode on the right, keeping it in quotes, for example 'drum B' or 'note mode'. Write None (no quotes) for nothing. Leave the button names on the left, the colons and the commas as they are. This is the default list:

    BUTTONS = {
    # button               mode it opens
    'Note':              'drum A',        # Note: drum sequencer A
    'Shift + Note':      'note mode',     # Shift + Note: Note mode

    'Chord':             'drum B',        # Chord: drum sequencer B
    'Shift + Chord':     None,

    'Custom':            'melodic A',     # Custom: melodic sequencer A
    'Shift + Custom':    'custom mode',   # Shift + Custom: Custom mode

    'Sequencer':         'melodic B',     # Sequencer: melodic sequencer B
    'Shift + Sequencer': 'lfo mode',      # Shift + Sequencer: LFO mode

    'Projects':          'melodic C',     # Projects: melodic sequencer C
    'Shift + Projects':  None,
}
  3. Choose the start tracks in START_TRACKS (1 = the first track in your Set). Selecting another track while a sequencer is shown still moves it, and the choice is saved in your Live Set. 
    START_TRACKS = {'drum A': 1, 'drum B': 2, 'melodic A': 3, 'melodic B': 4, 'melodic C': 5}
  4. Tune LFO mode's swings (at full depth; half depth swings half as far): volumes and sends in dB with a ceiling, every other control in % of its value. 
    LFO_DB_SWING = 10         # volumes and sends: dB below and above the fader
LFO_DB_CEILING = -10      # dB, the loudest an LFO makes a volume or send
LFO_SWING_PERCENT = 15    # every other control: % of its value, below and above
  5. Save, then reload the script: in Live, Settings → Link, Tempo & MIDI, set Control Surface to None, then back to Paunchpad.

Examples

Three drum sequencers

'Shift + Chord': 'drum C',

Shift + Chord now opens a third drum sequencer. Add 'drum C': 6 to START_TRACKS to start it on Track 6.

Note mode on its own button

'Shift + Note':     None,
'Projects':         'note mode',

Projects plays Note mode straight away, and melodic C moves to Shift + Projects.

Rules of thumbEach mode goes on one button only. Session always shows the clips. A sequencer keeps its track by name: rename 'melodic B' and the new name starts fresh.
Made a typo?Nothing breaks. A mistake in Config.py is skipped, the rest still works, and Live's Log.txt explains it (search for Paunchpad config).

Going further

If you're comfortable with a little Python, every speed, curve and colour is a named setting at the top of the script's files. For example, RANDOM_CURVE in Melodic.py sets how sparse Randomise is, SOFTEST_SWEEP_TIME in Faders.py sets how slow a soft hit moves a fader, and TAP_TIME in Custom.py sets how short a tap is. Reload the script after a change, as above.

Chapter 7

Faders

Press Volume, Pan, Sends or Device on the bottom row, and the grid becomes eight faders, one per column, with the bottom pad at the minimum. Press the button again, or Session, to go back to the clips.

Volume faders. Column 4 holds a micro value: its top pad is dimmed.
Colours
  • Volume: green. The top pad is 0 dB.
  • Pan: the track's colour, lit from the centre outwards.
  • Sends: the return track's colour. Scene buttons choose send A, B, …
  • Device: dark blue. Scene buttons choose the bank, ◀ ▶ the device.
⚡

Velocity sensitive

A fader glides to the pad you press. Hit hard and it gets there almost at once; hit softly and it moves slowly, up to 2.5 s for the full range. Press another pad mid-glide to redirect it.

¼

Micro values

Press the pad the fader is already on again: it steps through 4 finer values between that pad and the one below (¼, ½, ¾, full). The pad dims while it holds a micro value.

Just like the originalGlide speed and micro values follow Novation's own faders on the Launchpad X and Pro MK3. On/off-type parameters jump straight to the pad's value.
Chapter 8

Drum sequencer

Open with Note (sequencer A) or Chord (sequencer B) · leave with Session

A step sequencer for a Drum Rack, laid out like the Launchpad Pro MK3's own. Choose a sound on the drum pads, then tap steps to place it. The two drum sequencers work the same way, each on its own track.

Steps view: 32 steps on top, the drum pads bottom-left, tools bottom-right.
The four areas
  • Steps (top 4 rows): 32 steps. Steps holding the selected sound are in the track colour, the playhead is white, a longer gate is green.
  • Drum pads (bottom-left 4×4): the Drum Rack's sounds. The selected sound is white, and a pad flashes green when it plays.
  • Sound controls (bottom-right, rows 1–2): blue −, red + for the selected sound's first four parameters.
  • Throw pads (row 3): send the selected sound to a return, in the return's colour.
  • Note repeat (row 4): hold to repeat the sound at 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/32.

Velocity, Probability, Mutation, Micro Steps

These views edit one sound on one step. Tap a step to select it; it flashes white. The two bottom-right rows become an 8-level slider showing that note's value:

  • Velocity in the track colour
  • Probability purple
  • Mutation aqua
  • Micro Steps yellow: 6 positions inside the step

Sound controls

The blue (−) and red (+) pads move the selected sound's first four parameters: those of its own device, such as a Simpler's Filter Freq, Filter Res, Start and Length. Tap for a small nudge, hold to keep moving. The harder you hit, the faster it moves: about 25 s for the full range at the softest, 1.7 s at the hardest. Selecting a sound also shows its device in Live.

Throw pads

Hold a throw pad to send the selected sound to one of the Drum Rack's return chains, routed to a Set return. The harder you hit, the more you send: about a third at velocity 40, two thirds at 80, all of it at 127. Let go and the send returns to where it was. Shift + throw pad latches it on, and the pad flashes; tap it again to turn it off.

Setup, once per kitIn the Drum Rack, show the return chains section, add up to 4 return chains, and route each one's Audio To to a Set return. A throw pad stays dark until its return chain exists.

Recording automation

Press Shift + Record to record automation (Record pulses orange). While the pattern plays, what you do with the sound controls and throw pads is recorded into the pattern clip, as if you'd drawn it in Live's clip view. Filter sweeps, decay rides and dub throws become part of the pattern.

Automated pads glow like a rainbowA pad whose parameter has automation cycles through a fast rainbow: red, green and blue follow three sine waves, like an LFO. You can see at a glance what's moving in your pattern.

Note repeat

Hold an orange pad to play the selected sound repeated at 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16 or 1/32, in time with Live's tempo, keeping the velocity of your first hit. Hold two, and the last one pressed sets the rate. Repeats play only on the sequencer's own track: other armed tracks listening to the Launchpad are disarmed while you're here, and re-armed when you leave.

Pattern Settings

A 16-step loop at 1/16 (the selected rate is white).
  • Rows 1–4, the loop length: tap a step and the loop ends after it (1 to 32 steps). Peach steps are inside the loop.
  • Row 5, the step rate: 1/4, 1/4T, 1/8, 1/8T, 1/16, 1/16T, 1/32, 1/32T. Changing the rate keeps the number of steps.

At the default 1/16, 16 steps make 1 bar and 32 steps make 2 bars.

Melodic sequencer

Open with Custom, Sequencer or Projects · leave with Session

A Push-style note sequencer. Seven rows of notes from Live's key and scale, eight steps per page, and a row of page pads that shows the whole clip. There are three of them, each on its own track.

Notes in the track colour, the root row dimmed, the playhead green.
What you see
  • Notes in the track colour. Tap an empty pad to add a note, tap a note to remove it.
  • Root-note rows: the track colour, dimmed.
  • Playhead: a green column, white where it crosses a note.
  • Page pads (bottom row): white = page shown, green = page playing, dim = in the loop, dim track colour = outside the loop.
  • ▲ ▼ lit white: the loop has notes above / below the rows shown. Press them to go there.

Pattern Settings: loop, rate, key and scale

Rows 6–7 are a piano keyboard for the root note; row 8 the scale.
  • Rows 1–4: loop length, 1 to 32 steps.
  • Row 5: step rate.
  • Rows 6–7: the root note as a piano keyboard; the selected root is bright blue.

Key changes carry your clipsThe key and scale are Live's, shared by all three melodic sequencers. Changing them here also moves the notes of the clip playing on each melodic sequencer's track. A new root moves every note by the nearest interval; a new scale moves notes by scale step, so the 3rd stays the 3rd. Live's Undo puts them back.

Note mode

Open with Shift + Note · settings: Shift + Note again · leave with Session

The grid becomes an instrument for the selected track in Live, as in the Launchpad Pro MK3's Note mode. The track gets Live's hidden arm while you play, so select another track to play that one instead.

Chromatic layout, overlap 5, in C major.
  • Purple: the root note.
  • Blue: notes in the scale.
  • Unlit: other notes, still playable in the Chromatic layout.
  • Green: a pad you're pressing.
ButtonAction
▲ ▼Octave up / down
◀ ▶A semitone (Scale layout: a scale step)

Layouts

Chromatic

Every note, like a guitar neck. The default.

Scale

Only notes in the key, starting from the root, so you never play a wrong note.

Overlap 2–7

How far each row starts above the one below. 5 is the "guitar" layout, a 4th per row.

Sequential

Each row carries on where the one below ended. In the Scale layout, one row per octave.

Drum layout: four 4×4 blocks of drum pads.
Drum layout

If the selected track has a Drum Rack, the grid switches to four 4×4 blocks of drum pads automatically.

  • Yellow: a pad with a sound.
  • Blue: the selected pad. Pressing a pad selects it in the Drum Rack too.
  • Dim: an empty pad.

▲ ▼ move 16 pads, ◀ ▶ move 4.

Settings: Shift + Note while in Note mode.
Settings
  • Row 1: the Chromatic / Scale toggle (dim red = Chromatic, green = Scale).
  • Rows 2–3: the root note as a piano keyboard; the root is purple.
  • Rows 4–5: 16 scales. Root and scale are Live's, shared with the melodic sequencers.
  • Row 8: overlap 2–7, then Sequential on the last pad.
Chapter 11

Custom mode

Open with Shift + Custom · leave with Session or any bottom-row button

The 64 pads become sliders, each holding a value from 0 to 127 that its brightness shows. Link a pad to any control in Live and it becomes that control: moving the pad moves the knob, and moving the knob lights the pad.

Free pads in blue; linked pads in their track's colour.
Colours
  • Blue: a pad on its own, brighter as its value goes up.
  • Track colours: linked pads, in the colour of the track the control is on (a knob on return A in return A's colour).
  • White: the pad you're holding.

Pads keep their exact colour and only get brighter or dimmer with the value.

Play it with an LFO tooThe same pad in LFO mode moves the same control, with an LFO: this pad then moves the place the LFO swings around. Ableton's own MIDI mapping allows one mapping per parameter; Paunchpad sets values directly, so a control can have as many pads as you like.
One page for now, dozens to comeFor now Custom mode has a single page: the 64 slider pads described here. It's built to grow: each page keeps its own values and links, so more custom pages can be added, up to dozens of custom modes, each with its own layout and purpose.

Linking a pad to a control in Live

  1. Click a control in Live: any knob, fader or device parameter, a track's volume, pan or sends, even inside racks.
  2. Press Quantise + a pad. Live's status bar confirms, for example pad 5-5 -> Auto Filter: Frequency.
  3. Play it. The pad now moves that control, and with Live's automation arm on it records automation. Quantise + the same pad unlinks it.
Saved with your SetPad values and links are saved in the Live Set. Links follow their tracks even if you move them.
Not MIDI CCLinks drive Live's controls directly. They can't reach external gear: a script can't send MIDI into Live.
Chapter 12

LFO mode

Open with Shift + Sequencer · leave with Session or any top-row button

The Custom mode's 64 pads, but each one is a slow LFO: a wave (a sine, or one of seven other shapes) that gently moves the Live control it's linked to, around where you left it. Link a pad the same way (click a control in Live, then hold Quantise + pad), and play depth and rate with all the same gestures as the sliders.

Each pad in its track's colour, as bright as its depth, its light following its LFO. The bottom row picks the shape.
Two views, one tap apart
  • Depth (default): how far each LFO swings its control. Depth 0 = off. Pads are coloured as in Custom mode: the colour of the track the control sits on.
  • Rate: tap Quantise (dim white for depths, red for rates): now the pads set how fast each LFO moves, from 25 s a cycle to 4 s. Tap Quantise again to go back.
  • You see them move: a running LFO's pad dims and brightens with its wave. A sine breathes, a square blinks.
  • Hold Quantise + a pad to link it; a quick tap only switches views.

Chapter 13

Tracks, clips & saving

Each of the five sequencers keeps its own track, so you can have drums and melodies on the go and switch between them freely.

Choose a track

Select a MIDI track, in Live or with the track buttons, while a sequencer is shown. The sequencer locks to it, and selections made elsewhere don't move it.

Follows the playing clip

When a clip starts on the sequencer's track, the sequencer switches to it. When the track stops, the last clip stays.

Saved in the Set

Track choices are saved with the Live Set, stored on the tracks themselves, so they survive moving tracks and reopening the Set.

Coming back

Opening a sequencer selects its track (and clip) in Live, so Live always shows what the Launchpad is editing.

Troubleshooting

SymptomWhat to do
The Launchpad is dark or doesn't respondInput and output must be the LPProMK3 MIDI port, not DAW.
Drum pads or repeats are silentTurn on Track ✔ for the LPProMK3 MIDI input. The drum track needs an instrument, its input on All Ins, and Monitor on Auto.
Orange pads play once but don't repeatReload the script to get the current version.
An LFO pad doesn't move its controlLink it (hold Quantise + pad) and give it some depth: pads at depth 0 are still.
Live's undo list fills up, or automation fights an LFOThe LFOs move their controls continuously. Turn the LFO off (Shift + pad) on a control with recorded automation.
A change to the script doesn't show upSet Control Surface to None, then back to Paunchpad.
Checking what's runningPaunchpad writes to Live's log, ~/Library/Preferences/Ableton/Live 12.x/Log.txt. Search for Paunchpad: loaded to see which version is running.
